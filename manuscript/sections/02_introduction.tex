\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sustaining human exploration beyond Low Earth Orbit (LEO) requires the establishment of self-sustaining Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS) \citep{fu2016how}. Central to BLSS architecture are higher plants, which perform vital functions including carbon dioxide sequestration, oxygen generation, wastewater purification through transpiration, and dietary supplementation with fresh nutrients \citep{paul2017genetic,dixit2021persistence}. For decades, the model crucifer \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana} has served as the foundational benchmark for elucidating how plants perceive and adapt to extraterrestrial environments, notably altered gravity vectors, chronic low-dose cosmic radiation, and fluid-behavior anomalies \citep{choi2019variation,kruse2020spaceflight}.

Extensive transcriptomic and proteomic profiling archived in the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) has illuminated pervasive reprogramming of plant stress responses, hormone signaling, cell-wall remodeling, and epigenetic modifications during orbital flight \citep{barker2023meta,zhou2019epigenomics,olanrewaju2023integrative}. However, terrestrial plant biology has firmly established that plants do not function as isolated genetic entities; rather, they exist as ``holobionts'' intrinsically co-adapted with diverse microbial consortia comprising endophytic, phyllospheric, and rhizospheric bacteria and fungi \citep{dezelicourt2013rhizosphere,pieterse2014induced}. Beneficial endophytes confer pathogen resistance through induced systemic resistance (ISR), synthesize phytohormones such as indole-3-acetic acid (IAA), and alleviate abiotic stressors including drought and oxidative challenge. Conversely, alterations in host immunity in microgravity may render spaceflight crops vulnerable to opportunistic colonization by phytopathogens or human bacterial pathogens \citep{totsline2023microgravity,totsline2024simulated}.

Despite its paramount importance for space agriculture, characterization of the plant microbiome during spaceflight has faced significant experimental constraints. Launch mass limits, astronaut crew time limitations, and strict containment protocols often restrict dedicated metagenomic sampling. Consequently, prior studies of the International Space Station (ISS) microbiome have primarily focused on environmental air filters, surface swabs, and astronaut skin or gut microbiomes, leaving seedling-associated endophytic communities under-sampled.

Fortunately, modern high-throughput RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) performed on total plant tissue inherently captures a rich fraction of non-host transcripts \citep{park2019systematic}. When plant total RNA is extracted and sequenced without stringent poly(A) selection or when microbial transcripts share sequence features that evade depletion, hundreds of thousands of sequencing reads originate from bacteria, archaea, and fungi inhabiting the host tissues. In terrestrial genomics, such reads are traditionally categorized as ``unmapped dark matter'' and discarded during downstream differential expression analyses \citep{park2019systematic,ewels2020nf}.

In terrestrial agro-ecosystems, the foundational baseline of the seed microbiome has recently been deciphered at unprecedented scale. In a landmark meta-analysis spanning 3,190 seed samples across 50 plant species and 28 countries, \citet{simonin2022seed} established that seed microbiota are structured into a universal, highly conserved ``core'' fraction (dominated by \textit{Pantoea agglomerans}, \textit{Pseudomonas viridiflava}, \textit{Pseudomonas fluorescens}, and fungal taxa like \textit{Alternaria}) alongside a hyper-variable ``flexible'' fraction comprising rare, site-specific associates. Because the seed serves as the primary transgenerational inoculum for crop health, understanding whether the spaceflight environment preserves or disrupts this evolutionary core is of paramount urgency for long-duration life support. However, spaceflight seed biology operates under strict operational constraints: pre-flight chemical surface-sanitization (e.g., sodium hypochlorite or ethanol) strips the episphere, and growth occurs within tightly sealed hardware chambers subjected to microgravity, fluid-stagnation, and ionizing radiation.

Moreover, mining microbial signals from archival spaceflight datasets poses a profound computational challenge: the small-data, high-dimensionality curse ($N \ll P$). Spaceflight biological missions yield restricted sample sizes ($N \approx 10$--$50$) due to payload mass limits, whereas metagenomic and metatranscriptomic taxonomic features span dozens to hundreds of candidate taxa ($P$). Traditional deep learning architectures (e.g., multi-layer perceptrons, convolutional or graph networks) collapse or catastrophically overfit in small-$N$ regimes, while gradient boosted decision trees require complex hyperparameter tuning. Recently, \citet{hollmann2025accurate} demonstrated that tabular foundation models pre-trained on synthetic prior datasets—specifically Prior-Data Fitted Networks like TabPFN—achieve state-of-the-art predictive performance and well-calibrated Bayesian inference on small tabular data in a single forward pass without hyperparameter optimization.

In this study, we capitalize on this bioinformatic and deep learning convergence by conducting a systematic meta-transcriptomic recovery and microbial profiling of archival \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana} spaceflight datasets deposited in NASA OSDR \citep{barker2023meta}. By benchmarking observed spaceflight communities against the global 50-species seed baseline \citep{simonin2022seed} and applying the TabPFN tabular foundation model \citep{hollmann2025accurate}, we address fundamental questions regarding spaceflight microbiome dynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Do surface-sanitized \textit{Arabidopsis} seedlings grown in closed spaceflight hardware harbor persistent, detectable microbial communities, and how do they compare against the universal terrestrial seed core identified by \citet{simonin2022seed}?
    \item How are spaceflight communities partitioned between intrinsic seed-borne endophytes, oligotrophic blooms (\textit{Ralstonia pickettii}), stress-adapted probiotics (\textit{Weizmannia ginsengihumi}), and extrinsic anthropogenic or hardware commensals?
    \item How does hardware containment (closed BRIC canisters vs.\ open illuminated APEX systems) drive ecological filtering, and can tabular foundation models (TabPFN) accurately decode these environmental signatures from ultra-sparse microbial matrices?
    \item What are the predictive implications for agricultural seed stocks and candidate crop species planned for future lunar and Martian Bioregenerative Life Support Systems?
\end{enumerate}
